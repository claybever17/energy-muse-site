Frequency guide

One frequency, held steady.

A frequency generator is a small device that emits one specific frequency and keeps emitting it. You put it in a room and leave it there. This page explains what each of the seven is for, where to place it, and why we build them fixed rather than adjustable.

The Seven Frequencies Hear the Room Re-tune

The seven frequencies.

Start with the one whose description sounds like the thing you actually want more of. There is no wrong first choice, and none of them cancels another out.

Frequency generators are not medical devices and are not a substitute for healthcare. Experiences vary.

Where to put it.

The room you use most

A generator works on a space, not a person, so it belongs where you actually spend time. A living room, a desk, a bedside table.

Out in the open

Not in a drawer or behind a stack of books. It wants a clear surface at roughly waist height or above, near the middle of the room rather than a corner.

Left alone

Switch it on and leave it. The point of a fixed frequency is that it does not need managing — consistency is the whole practice.

Why fixed, not adjustable.

Most frequency devices let you dial through a range. That sounds like more for your money, and it is the opposite. A device that can produce any frequency has to compromise the circuit that produces each one, so the signal is weaker and the radius smaller.

Every Energy Muse generator is built around a crystal oscillator and a copper tensor coil cut for one frequency, matched with a specific sacred geometric shape. Nothing about it is a compromise between seven jobs, because it only has one. That is also why people end up with more than one rather than one that does everything.

Common questions.

Can I run more than one at a time?

Yes, and many people do — the Formulas are built on exactly that idea, layering three in one room. Run alone, a frequency shifts a moment; run together, they hold an environment.

Does it need to be on all the time?

No. Consistency matters more than duration. Many people run one through the working day, or overnight, and switch it off the rest of the time.

How big a space does one cover?

One device is designed for one room. If you want a whole home to feel consistent, that is what the systems are for rather than one device working harder.

Will I hear it?

Some people describe a subtle hum, most describe nothing audible at all. What people tend to notice is the room, not the device.
