The reminder that comes with you.

A bracelet works because you meet it a hundred times a day without meaning to. This page covers getting the size right, wearing more than one, and keeping natural stone on elastic looking like the day it arrived.

Getting the size right.

Measure your wrist with a tape measure, or a strip of paper against a ruler, snug but not tight. Then take the size at or just above that number — beads take up room on the inside of the curve, so a bracelet always wears slightly smaller than it measures.

6¾″Small. Sits close, stays put under a sleeve.21 beads + rondelle
7½″The most common size, and the safest guess.23 beads + rondelle
8″Relaxed. Moves on the wrist, good for stacking.25 beads + rondelle

Between two sizes? Take the larger one. Elastic is far happier slightly loose than slightly stretched, and a bracelet worn at tension is a bracelet that will break.

Wearing more than one.

Stacking is not about quantity. Two or three pieces that mean different things read as deliberate; six read as a collection you could not choose between.

Works

  • One intention per piece, so the stack says something specific.
  • Mixing bead sizes — a fine strand next to an 8mm gives the stack a rhythm.
  • Repeating one metal tone across the stack to tie it together.
  • Splitting across both wrists rather than loading one.

Doesn’t

  • Stacking so tightly the pieces cannot move — that is what abrades the stone.
  • Wearing softer stones like malachite or selenite against quartz, which will scratch them.
  • Adding a piece because you own it rather than because it is for something.

Everyday care.

  1. Roll it on, don’t yank it. Pulling a beaded bracelet over your knuckles stretches the elastic every single time. Roll it up the hand instead.
  2. Take it off for water, chemicals and the gym. Showers, pools, hot tubs, perfume, hairspray, sunscreen and cleaning products all shorten the life of both the cord and the stone. Put it on last when dressing, take it off first.
  3. Wipe it, don’t soak it. A dry or barely damp soft cloth. Never submerge a piece with malachite, selenite, turquoise or pyrite in it.
  4. Store it flat and apart. Not in a heap in a dish. Hard stones scratch soft ones, and a bracelet stored under tension is a bracelet under tension for months.

Natural stone varies piece to piece — colour, banding and inclusions differ between beads and between bracelets. That is the material, not a fault.

Choose a piece.

Start with what you want more of, and let the stone follow from that rather than the other way round.
